Assalamu alaikum, and welcome to Connecting Letters, Reading Words.

My name is Wafa Abdul Latheef. I’m an additional language tutor, and I’m studying at the University of Wollongong in Dubai. I designed this course for one reason: in many classrooms, learners can name every Arabic letter, but when the letters join into a word, they stall. And teachers deserve practical, evidence-informed ways to help.

[Slide: the four outcomes, one per line] Over five weeks, you will do four things. First, work out where a learner’s difficulty actually sits. Second, design a short digital modelling sequence that makes joining visible. Third, evaluate practice tools for feedback, accessibility and data protection. And fourth, plan two weeks of real classroom use.

[Slide: weekly rhythm] Here is how each week works. Every week has a short video, a few readings, and no more than three required tasks. You’ll join a 45-minute live clinic on Teams. It’s optional, and it’s recorded and captioned. Everything is online and self-paced, about three hours a week.

[Slide: assessments] You’ll complete four tasks. The first is a practice task that carries no marks. The other three build on each other, so everything you make is something you can use in your own classroom.

[Slide: support] If you’re stuck, message me through the course page. I reply within 48 hours on weekdays. If something stops you from accessing the course, tell the helpdesk and we’ll fix it. And if you miss a couple of tasks, I’ll get in touch with options, not pressure.

[Slide: first steps] Your first steps are small. Watch this video, complete the five-minute readiness survey, and post a 60-second introduction. Tell us your grade level and one reading challenge you’d like to solve.

I’m looking forward to learning with you. Let’s get started.

Course concept map

Learning outcomes

LO1 · A1

🔍 Analyse

Spot what is behind a learner’s reading difficulty.

LO2 · A2

🎨 Design

Build a clear, accessible modelling sequence.

LO3 · A3

💡 Evaluate

Judge tools for feedback, access and data.

LO4 · A4

🏁 Plan

Plan two weeks of classroom use.

In scope

Arabic letter joining and word reading for Grades 1–4, interactive visual modelling, corrective feedback, repeated practice and progress monitoring, accessibility and data protection in tool choice.

Out of scope

Full Arabic curriculum design, grammar and comprehension, building software or AI systems, and training children directly.

Glossary

Frequently asked questions

What if I fall behind?

Message the educator. Options include an extension, a catch-up summary or an alternative format.

Can I use AI tools?

You may use them as tools. Acknowledge any AI use when you submit, and keep learner data out of them. The submission form includes this declaration.

Can I use real learner work?

Only fictional or anonymised samples.

What if I miss the live clinic?

Clinics are optional, recorded and captioned, so you can catch up any time.

What if I have a technical or accessibility problem?

Tell the course helpdesk. You will hear back within one working day, and unresolved issues go to the course coordinator.

Do I need technical skills?

No. Each tool has a guide, and every task has a non-AI alternative.

Is there a certificate?

Yes, a certificate of completion.

Join the course 🎓

Who can enrol

  • You teach or support Arabic in Grades 1–4 in a GCC school.
  • You can give about 3 hours a week for five weeks, plus 45 minutes for orientation.
  • You have a phone or laptop with internet access and can read English instructions.
  • You will use only fictional or anonymised learner samples.

No prior digital-literacy training is needed. Tell us about access needs such as screen readers or captions when you enrol.
